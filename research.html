Research & Projects

Our research examines how mental health problems develop in young people, how brain–body processes may contribute, and how to assess risk and test interventions in schools and everyday life.

Epidemiology and developmental risk Interoception and brain–body mechanisms School-based suicide and self-injury prevention Digital assessment and interventions

Epidemiology and developmental risk

We study how common self-injury and suicidal thoughts and behaviors are among young people, and how developmental, cognitive, and environmental factors relate to risk.

Cognitive and developmental neuroscience of suicide and non-suicidal self-injury

Examines cognitive and developmental processes to inform prevention and intervention.

Adolescent Health Enhancing Long-term Plan (A-HELP)

Studies the prevalence of self-injury and suicidal thoughts and behaviors in China, along with individual and environmental factors.

Interoception and brain–body mechanisms

We study signals from the brain, heart, lungs, and gastrointestinal tract, and how those signals relate to what people feel. Three lines of work connect these measurements to mental health research and intervention development.

See the lab’s interoception review and other papers

Measure brain–body interactions
We combine brain measures such as EEG and fMRI with recordings of heartbeat, breathing, and stomach activity.
Track bodily sensations in everyday life
We use digital body maps and brief, repeated check-ins to study where people feel sensations and how those sensations change over time.
Develop and evaluate interventions
We develop biofeedback, non-invasive neuromodulation guided by physiological feedback, and psychological interventions informed by interoception.

School-based suicide and self-injury prevention

We develop prevention programs for students and teachers. Our student program includes awareness of bodily signals alongside emotion regulation and interpersonal skills.

Universal prevention of non-suicidal self-injury

Focuses on interpersonal relationships, emotion regulation, and awareness of bodily signals.

Life Gatekeeper training

Helps teachers recognize students at risk and develop skills to respond.

Featured study

Life Gatekeeper Training in Schools

In a cluster-randomized trial across 84 schools in China, teachers who received Life Gatekeeper Training reported greater confidence and willingness to intervene. Differences were still evident at 12 months.

  • Trial results in Nature Health (2026)
  • Study protocol in Trials (2024)

Digital assessment and interventions

We study speech-based approaches to assessing suicide risk and test digital approaches to providing support, including apps and virtual reality.

Virtual reality for mental health

Explores virtual reality as a way to support young people’s mental health and well-being.

Training for Emotional Adaptation (TEA)

An online program through Redbud Mental Wellness that draws on emotion regulation and internet-delivered cognitive behavioral therapy.

Speech-based suicide risk research

Studies the use of speech analysis and language models to assess suicide risk among adolescents.

Real-time monitoring and intervention

Examines the long-term effectiveness of app-based support and how it can be tailored to individual needs.
